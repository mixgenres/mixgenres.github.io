# Arabic Takht score pass

Date: 2026-10-07  
Scope: written score, instrument roles, techniques and section pattern assignments; no playback or mix review.

## Finding

The default `arabic::takht` score did not use the ney statement literally everywhere, but the concern was justified. Before this change, the ney statement was selected in the taqsim, intro and response; the same eight even attacks were selected for oud and qanun in several ensemble sections. Existing generated variations appeared in some verses and solo sections, but did not create the instrument-to-instrument phrase relationship promised by “heterophonic takht.” The recording form also failed to mark its taqsim as a ney feature, leaving every instrument in that section.

## Changes

The score now exposes the ney alone for the taqsim, retains the chamber ensemble for composed passages, and gives the oud an accompanied feature later in the form. The recording arrangement pins a style-owned pattern per instrument in each named section, so the student sample does not depend on a random pattern choice to show the phrase relationship. New style-local studies add a breath-grouped ney phrase for taqsim, a short ney response beneath the oud feature, a staggered kamanja response with slide inflection, a more spacious oud support figure and a separate oud answer, and qanun ornaments placed between melodic entries. Each instrument has a distinct role and section-use assignment; the cells are learning studies, not transcriptions of the recording.

The takht violin technique list no longer includes `celtic-ornament`. It now names ornament, ornamented slide and microtonal inflection, with legato as the default for the melodic part. The ensemble description now distinguishes improvisatory taqsim from composed passages and describes heterophony as related, staggered lines rather than unison copies.

## Evidence and limits

Cal Poly’s [Arab Music Ensemble overview](https://ame.calpoly.edu/ensemble/) describes maqam and iqa, takht personnel, taqsim improvisation and composed forms such as sama‘i and muwashshah. The University of Würzburg’s [Takht instrument overview](https://www.musikwissenschaft.uni-wuerzburg.de/musikinstrumente/organologie/doku/syrische-tonspuren/tonspuren-audiomat/hoerstationen/takht/) identifies the qanun, oud, nay, kamanja and riqq as core ensemble instruments. The University of Miami’s [Arabic violin technique thesis](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/doctoral/A-guide-to-Arabic-violin-technique/991031447726402976) is a targeted source for the violin technique audit.

The score is an educational arrangement built around the selected recording reference, not a note-for-note reconstruction. A musician familiar with the relevant regional takht practice should review the exact phrase contours and maqam inflections before those cells are presented as authoritative repertoire.
