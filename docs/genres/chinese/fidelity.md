# Chinese genre fidelity pass

Status: **partial implementation, validation open**. This dossier follows [the genre fidelity pass specification](../../genre-fidelity-pass-spec.md). It distinguishes authored patterns from generated drills and does not claim a listening pass.

## Baseline and scope

Eight styles were audited: Jiangnan Sizhu, guqin, guzheng, pipa, Jingju, Cantonese ensemble, Chaozhou, and suona chuida. The app exposes 11 Chinese instrument models. Seven styles have an exact local catalog match; Jiangnan Sizhu also has an exact local source file whose Chinese artist/title needed an explicit inventory alias. Three styles (Jingju, Cantonese ensemble, and Chaozhou) have separated-accompaniment mix evidence. The reference pass now examines four distinct ensemble contexts—Jiangnan Sizhu, Cantonese ensemble, Chaozhou, and a suona-and-orchestra performance—along with the guqin, guzheng, and pipa solo references. The suona recording is an orchestral adaptation of a traditional piece, so it is useful for the lead's range/articulation and broad blend review but is not a traditional chuida production target. A file match does not establish a timecoded musical analysis.

Before this pass, most source cells were tiny placeholder motifs and defaulted to one static D5 anchor with near-identical 4/4 song form. The generated catalog hid the gap: across all 55 genres, 14,099 source-labelled bodies included 11,863 derived drills, while the true folder-authored count was 2,230 cells, with 1,904 of 2,078 instrument/style pairs having one and 29 having none. Only 197 authored cells contained pitches. After this Chinese pass, all 21 active Chinese instrument/style pairs have two authored body studies each; this does not close the notation, mechanics, or listening findings below.

## Findings

- **CHN-P1-01 — single-cell instrument teaching (all eight styles).** Source: the original `cells` in [Chinese catalog.ts](../../../src/data/genres/chinese/catalog.ts), audited against compiled per-measure pattern assignments. Observed: these cells commonly used three repeated placeholder notes or generic eighth-note accompaniment. Correction: add local phrase-level studies with pitches, instrument-specific onsets and articulations; separate statement, answer, support, and cue roles. The count criterion now passes: all 21 active player/style pairs have two authored body choices, and every Chinese sample song compiles with at least two selected pattern IDs per lane. Phrase authenticity across each complete form still needs timecoded musical review.
- **CHN-P1-02 — regional personnel blurred.** Source: [Oxford on Chinese heterophonic ensemble practice](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/52498/chapter-abstract/421531033) and [UMBC's Chaozhou xianshi overview](https://www2.umbc.edu/eol/dujunco/xsy.html). Jiangnan previously exposed guzheng as a default player. It is removed from that style; the available reduction uses erhu, dizi, and pipa. The instrument catalog lacks yangqin and sanxian, so the reduction records this gap rather than filling it with an unrelated model. Chaozhou removes dizi and records unavailable erxian/tihu/yehu/sanxian models. Acceptance: style ensembles contain only documented local instruments or explicitly declared, reviewed reductions.
- **CHN-P1-03 — Jingju percussion reduced to generic pulse.** Source: [Grinnell's Jingju ensemble instrument notes](https://omeka-s.grinnell.edu/s/MusicalInstruments/page/beijing). New paigu drag/flam/ghost/roll and sparse gong cue material now has different functions. The engine lacks bangu, cymbal, clapper, and complete luogu-jing cue notation. Acceptance: each rendered phrase preserves cue points and rests against an identified vocal or instrumental phrase.
- **CHN-P1-04 — fake confidence from derived drills.** Provenance now separates folder-authored cells from local answer/reduction/technique patterns in the audit. Acceptance: all coverage reporting keeps these counts distinct.
- **CHN-P1-05 — production evidence mislabeled.** Solo instrumental Guqin, Pipa, and Guzheng recordings were tagged `original-with-vocals`; they are now tagged `original-recording`. They remain excluded from separated-accompaniment mix calibration. Acceptance: source labels match the actual evidence type and no original recording is used as a Demucs accompaniment target.
- **CHN-P1-06 — Jiangnan stereo image too narrow.** Source: the local 335-second `Huanle Ge` recording, matched at 75–105 s against generated seconds 0–30. The original had side/mid RMS ratio 0.786, crest 18.12 dB, and RMS −19.10 dBFS; the generated mix measured 0.243, 14.30 dB, and −15.49 dBFS. The initial stage measured width 0.028. A style-specific role-width/pan adjustment raised it to 0.243, but the remaining width difference (−0.543) is still large. Spectral distance is 0.274 and centroid ratio 0.970; these are diagnostics, not similarity scores. The renderer did not clip. Acceptance: inspect individual instrument placement, source decorrelation and room; compare additional matched sections, then listen and document whether the stage feels like a small Jiangnan ensemble. No human listening verdict is claimed.
- **CHN-P2-01 — production target and loudness interpretation.** Across original Huanle Ge windows at 75, 150, and 250 seconds, median RMS was −19.49 dBFS, crest 18.71 dB, low-band share 0.0005, high-band share 0.2231, and side/mid ratio 0.7734. These describe that recording and must not be copied as album loudness targets. The generated comparison window was 3.61 dB louder; preserve this as a review point rather than applying a blind global gain correction. Acceptance: compare at matched perceived loudness across multiple sections and listen for transient shape, note density, and lead/support balance.
- **CHN-P2-02 — suona reference scope.** A direct MediaHuman download now supplies the previously missing `Bai Niao Chao Feng` reference (2022 National Orchestra New Year Concert; four named suona performers with Chinese orchestras). Three 12-second album windows were measured at 0, 164.67, and 305.82 seconds. Their median RMS is −29.71 dBFS, crest 17.06 dB, and side/mid ratio 0.5458. These original-master measurements are descriptive only: the source is an orchestral adaptation, not a village-processional chuida ensemble; they do not automatically change the app's mix defaults. Acceptance: create a timecoded player map, compare a traditional wind/percussion performance, and audition the generated arrangement before calibrating its role balance.

## Implemented material

`src/data/genres/chinese/studies.ts` supplies pitched examples: Jiangnan erhu statement/dizi answer/pipa support; guqin open, stopped, harmonic and pitch-shading phrases; guzheng localized bend and yaozhi passages; pipa tan-tiao and lunzhi/sao studies; Jingju aria, jinghu response, paigu cue and gong punctuation; Cantonese gaohu/dizi/plucked responses; Chaozhou bowed/plucked variation; and Chuida suona calls with paigu/gong processional cues. These are original teaching studies, not note-for-note transcriptions.

Chinese studies are assigned only to their own style IDs. Body studies now have role-appropriate song sections, and the compiler was spot-checked across all eight Chinese catalog songs; each generated a sheet without an instrument membership or harmonic-plan exception. Full time-coded per-player listening maps and broader variation beyond the two-study minimum remain open.

## Genre-local evidence and limits

The Oxford chapter on heterophonic practice and the UMBC Chaozhou source support shared-melody variation and the Chaozhou ensemble boundary. Grinnell and Smithsonian Folkways support Jingju cue percussion and Chuida wind/percussion context. The source manifest contains links and scope. These sources guide arrangement design; they do not prove that the current render sounds authentic.
