# All-genres pattern and technique pass

Date: 2026-10-07  
Scope: notation, instrument roles, style-owned patterns, section assignments and pedagogical studies across the catalog. Playback rendering, mix calibration, loudness, source separation, and listening review are deferred.

## Catalog-wide structural audit

The strengthened default-score audit traverses 55 genres, all 420 styles, and 2,466 instrument tracks. It resolves the patterns actually assigned by section and compares their body event shapes: onset and duration layout, hit type, articulation, and available pitch, ornament, technique, and tuplet events. It also reports dominant-shape share, style/instrument technique vocabulary that is not literally present in the style-owned body patterns, sample-support tracks, and any foreign pattern assignments. This catches cases where different IDs conceal identical material, and where one tiny fill makes an otherwise repeated song appear varied.

The current run finds **zero tracks with only one or zero body event shapes**, **zero tracks without body assignments**, and **zero foreign or out-of-style assignments**. The 314 sample-support tracks added to satisfy sample-song ensemble size each have at least two event shapes. They are explicitly marked `sample-support`: roster accompaniment, not evidence that the style owns that instrument or that the part teaches its idiomatic technique. Where available, support timing is derived from same-style rhythm material, while instrument-specific mechanics are copied only from a source for that same instrument.

The audit retains 42 low-variation prompts for human review: 37 are sample-support lanes with a high dominant-shape share, and five are style-owned continuous roles. The five are Indian classical thumri/tanpura, Carnatic kriti/tanpura, ragam-tanam-pallavi/tanpura, steppe kargyraa/morin-khuur, and weird polymetric/synth. Their repetition may fit the role (drone, modal support, or ostinato), but the prompt remains visible so a reviewer can assess the local treatment rather than assuming repetition is correct.

There are also 114 technique-cue review rows, containing 145 unmatched literal cue strings. These are conservative vocabulary comparisons against style-owned instrument body patterns; they are **review prompts, not 145 confirmed missing techniques**. Wording may differ, and some cues describe arrangement, phrase behavior, or rhythmic function rather than a physical technique. They require semantic classification and musician/source review before any are treated as defects. A separate earlier [genre pedagogy audit](genre-pedagogy-audit.md) found 1,904 instrument/style pairs with one authored body cell, 29 with none, and 76 technique cues lacking renderer gesture mappings. Those renderer-gesture findings are distinct from this literal-match audit; neither is closed merely by creating more sample-song variation.

## Changes in this pass

`src/data/genres/_shared/sampleSongDevelopment.ts` adds explicit style-owned section cells for every one-pattern track in the score audit. The cells are keyed to the exact genre/style, instrument, role and pedagogical function. They are composed as contrasting phrase/section alternatives rather than mechanically copying a whole-song pattern. Examples include a displaced fragment response for glitch ambient, a distinct piano answer for big-band horn phrases, three-beat bass motion for vals, a swara-like sarangi phrase for tillana, and separate singer versus morin-khuur roles in khöömei.

The changes preserve several important distinctions. Gamelan gongs punctuate cycles and section transitions while interlocking melody belongs to the ensemble's other instruments. Gagaku shō remains a sustained chordal breath layer. Tanpura changes its pluck cycle while maintaining the drone. Khöömei overtone production remains a vocal technique; it is not attributed to the bowed morin khuur.

The event-shape audit is the structural acceptance check, with a minimum of two distinct body shapes per track and a section-level assignment check. Distinct IDs alone do not pass. Intentional continuous roles use contextual material rather than unrelated notes or rhythms. `canonicalPatternSection()` also recognizes sonata movements as verse material and numbered interludes as bridges, allowing section studies in the prepared-piano form to reach their intended sections. Explicit sample assignments are locked against phrase variation replacing the selected section cell.

## Evidence used

- The UNESCO [Gamelan inscription](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/gamelan-01607) describes simultaneous melody and interlocking instrumental parts. The University of Hawai‘i [Javanese Gamelan program](https://manoa.hawaii.edu/music/about-us/ensembles/gamelan/) explicitly teaches colotomic structure and types of improvisation; this supports cycle-aware gong roles rather than a generic drum loop.
- Stanford's [Gagaku Shō guide](https://gagaku.stanford.edu/en/woodwinds/sho/) describes the shō's sustained aitake chord function and four-measure phrase structure. The new cell changes sustained chord-breath timing without treating the shō as a percussion or melodic lead part.
- UNESCO's [Traditional Mongolian Khöömei entry](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-mongolian-art-of-khoomei-00396) describes the singer's fundamental and overtone line. UNESCO's [morin khuur entry](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-music-of-the-morin-khuur-00068?lang=en) describes its two-string bowed-fiddle identity and the simultaneous main tone/overtones. The catalog keeps those vocal and instrumental functions separate.
- The [Library of Congress fiddle glossary](https://www.loc.gov/static/collections/henry-reed-fiddle-tunes/about-this-collection/related-resources.html) and [Henry Reed tune notes](https://www.loc.gov/item/afcreed000171/) distinguish concrete bowing, double-stop and open-string practice from broad style labels. This distinction informs the remaining technique-cue audit, which is not yet complete.
- The University of Michigan [Samba Residency](https://ii.umich.edu/lacs/about-us/brazil-initiative/arts-and-culture-network/samba-residency.html) identifies the teleco-teco timeline and distinct surdo roles as ensemble foundations. Grinnell's [surdo instrument entry](https://omeka-s.grinnell.edu/s/MusicalInstruments/item/1240) documents first/second surdo beat roles and open/muted tones. This is a useful model for the still-pending instrument-by-instrument rhythm audit.

## Status and limits

This is a catalog-wide structural and data-shape pass, not proof that every event is idiomatic for a particular regional school, performer, or recording. The automated checks do not establish note-by-note correctness, instrument mechanics, tuning, or that a named technique is truly represented in notation. Newly authored cells are original local studies where they develop source material; they need musician review before being presented as authoritative repertoire. The 145 unmatched cue strings remain a semantic audit queue, not a claim that all techniques have been fixed.

No audio-layer checks, rendering, listening, mix calibration, loudness analysis, or source separation were run. The five-recording-per-style acquisition and verification workflow is documented in [the genre reference pass](genre-reference-pass.md); this structural audit did not acquire new MP3 references.
